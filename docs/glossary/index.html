Plain definitions of the grading and planning terms we use across Ed Technolog, with the sources they rest on.

Mastery grading · Standards alignment

Mastery grading

Mastery grading reports whether a student can do a specific skill yet, rather than averaging every score from a term into one number. Students get more than one chance to show a skill, so the grade reflects where they are now, not where they started.

Mastery grading, standards-based grading and competency-based grading are related but not the same thing. Competency-based grading, for example, is one kind of standards-based grading that borrows parts of mastery grading and adds its own rules for how course grades are set.

Source: Our post Mastery grading without the headache: “Mastery grading asks a simple question: can the student do the thing yet? It replaces averages that hide growth with a clearer picture of what a student knows.” The Nebraska Center for Transformative Teaching, in What is Competency-based Grading?, describes competency-based grading as “a type of standards-based grading that incorporates aspects of mastery grading”, with “multiple opportunities to demonstrate mastery”.

Why it matters for teachers: You can start small, with one course, four or five skills and a scale of three or four levels, and Markbook runs mastery and percentage grades side by side so you can pilot it before committing.

Standards alignment

Standards alignment means tying each unit and lesson you plan to the specific standards for your grade and subject, so you can see which standards a plan covers.

Source: Our Plan a Unit guide: “Add standards from the built-in list for your grade and subject.” Our product page lists PlanBoard’s “Built-in standards library for US state frameworks”.

Why it matters for teachers: In PlanBoard you pick standards as you build the unit, instead of matching them up afterwards, so the plan stays tied to what students are expected to learn.
